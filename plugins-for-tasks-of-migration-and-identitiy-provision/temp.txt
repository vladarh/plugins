##  The Timeline Block: Point-In-Time Constraints
Your recovered data coordinates are split across two different recovery metrics:
Database Point (WAL-G): The database engine recovers data up to the exact transaction record of your last uploaded WAL segment. Because PostgreSQL continuously pushes WAL files as they fill or via a short timeout loop, your database state is nearly real-time (often within minutes or seconds of the crash).
Attachment Point (rsync): The filesystem works from the snapshot of your last completed replication execution loop. If your cron container triggers rsync every 15 minutes, you face a maximum 15-minute file discrepancy window.
The Index Cache Block: To prevent file pointer fragmentation during transfer, your rsync architecture must actively block the transfer of the caches/indexes directory. When the DR Jira 10 container mounts the PVC for the first time, it intentionally starts with no search index, automatically triggering an internal background reindex task. [1]
